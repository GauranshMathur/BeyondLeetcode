This is Dijkstra's algorithm. Keep the best total found so far for each node, starting with `A` at 0, and a min-heap of (total, node) pairs. Take the smallest pair. If its total is more than the best known for that node, it is stale, left in the heap by a later, cheaper offer: skip it. Otherwise its total is final, and for each edge from the node the total plus the edge's weight is offered to the edge's end: if it beats the best known for that node, record it and push a new pair. When the heap is empty, the best totals are the answers. `edge` stores each edge's weight in a map and keeps the lower one on a repeat.

Every edge pushes at most one pair, so the heap never holds more than E + 1 pairs; each is pushed and popped once at O(log E), which is O(log V) since E is at most V². That makes the search O((V + E) log V) in the **worst case**, with O(V + E) space. The method needs every weight to be 1 or more, which the Problem promises: with a negative weight a node's total could still fall after it was taken from the heap.

FRRouting's candidate list deletes and re-adds a node when a cheaper route is found ([ospfd/ospf_spf.c, lines 1515 to 1518 (FRRouting frr-10.0)](https://github.com/FRRouting/frr/blob/03a143cd49ed1b190cb38f48a31ef4d71fa01a02/ospfd/ospf_spf.c#L1515-L1518)), where this solution leaves the old pair in the heap and skips it later. Both give the same costs.
