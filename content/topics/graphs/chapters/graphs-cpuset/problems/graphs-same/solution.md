This is the union-find of the Chapter. Every node starts as its own parent. `find` follows parents to the root and, on the way, points each node it passes at its grandparent. `union` finds both roots; if they differ, it points the root of the lower tree at the root of the higher one. `edge` calls `union` on its two ends (and still keeps its list for the earlier commands). `same` compares two roots, and `groups` counts the nodes that are their own parent.

With union by rank and path shortening, each `find` and `union` costs O(α(V)) **amortised**, where α is the inverse Ackermann function, which the kernel's document says "can be roughly considered a constant time complexity for practical purposes" ([union_find.rst, lines 38 to 41](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/adc218676eef25575469234709c2d87185ca223a/Documentation/core-api/union_find.rst#L38-L41)); a single `find` can take longer, but the cost evens out over a sequence of operations. The document calls the bound average, and this Problem labels it amortised. `groups` here is O(V). The space is O(V). Answering `same` by searching the graph each time would cost O(V + E) per question.

The kernel's `uf_find` and `uf_union` do the same two steps ([lib/union_find.c, lines 13 to 48](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/adc218676eef25575469234709c2d87185ca223a/lib/union_find.c#L13-L48)).
