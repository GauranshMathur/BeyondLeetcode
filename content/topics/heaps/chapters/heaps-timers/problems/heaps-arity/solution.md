The only changes are the three places that used the number 2. The parent of position `p` is `(p - 1) / D`, the first child of `i` is `D*i + 1`, and `init` starts at `(n - 2) / D`. To find the smallest child the move down compares the children one by one, `c - 1` comparisons for `c` children. Moving up is unchanged apart from the parent formula.

A heap of `n` items has about log base `D` of `n` rows, so with larger `D` the climb in `push` is shorter, and the descent in `pop` is shorter too but costs up to `D - 1` comparisons per row. For 21 items a binary heap needs 5 rows (1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 = 31 slots), and a heap with four children needs 3 (1 + 4 + 16 = 21 slots). With `D` fixed, both `push` and `pop` are O(log n) comparisons in the **worst case**: a pop makes up to `D - 1` comparisons on each of about log base `D` of `n` rows.
