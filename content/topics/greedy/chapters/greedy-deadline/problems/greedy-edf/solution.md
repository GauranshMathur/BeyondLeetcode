Sort the jobs by deadline. Let `clock = 0` and `worst = 0`. For each job in that order: add its run time to `clock`, then set `worst = max(worst, clock - deadline)`. Print `worst`.

**Why earliest deadline first is safe.** Take any order and suppose two jobs next to each other, A then B, have `deadline(A) > deadline(B)` (A is later than B in the sorted order, but runs first). Let `t` be the time A starts. Before the swap, A finishes at `t + run(A)` and B at `t + run(A) + run(B)`. After the swap, B finishes at `t + run(B)` and A at `t + run(A) + run(B)`. Jobs elsewhere finish at the same times as before. B now finishes earlier, so its tardiness cannot be larger. A now finishes at the time B used to, and its deadline is later than B's, so `finish - deadline` for A is below what it was for B before the swap. The largest tardiness after the swap is therefore at most the largest before it. Swapping such neighbours until none is left gives the sorted order, so the sorted order is no worse than any other order. Equal deadlines: swapping neighbours with equal deadlines changes the finish time of each, but the later one finishes at the same time, so the largest of the two tardiness values is unchanged.

**Cost.** Sorting is O(n log n) in the **worst case** (a comparison sort), the walk is O(n), and the extra space is O(n) for the sorted copy (or O(1) if the sort is in place).
