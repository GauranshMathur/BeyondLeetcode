Fill the two rows as in `edit`. After each row is finished, if its smallest cell is more than `LIMIT`, stop. At the end print the last cell of the last row if it is at most `LIMIT`, and `over` if it is more.

**Why it is right.** Each cell of a row is the cheapest of three moves, each of which adds a cost of at least 1 to a cell of the previous row or to a cell to its left in the same row, so a row's smallest cell is never below the smallest of the row before. The answer is a cell of the last row, so it is at least the smallest cell of any finished row. When that smallest cell is already more than `LIMIT`, the answer is too, and printing `over` is right. When the loop finishes, the last cell is the true cost.

**Cost.** In the **worst case** nothing is saved: a distance at or just under the limit needs every row, O((m + 1) × (n + 1)) time, and space is still O(m + 1). The saving is for a distance far over the limit, which is found after fewer rows. PostgreSQL's own version goes further and keeps only a band of each row near the diagonal, as the Chapter describes.
